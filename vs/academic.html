Academic Projects

Autonomous RC Car System

Supervisor: Prof. Suman Banerjee, Dept. of CS, UW-Madison | Sep 2020 - Dec 2021

In this project, we worked on developing an open-source, affordable, and high-performance autonomous vehicle testbed. Specifically, we aimed to enable technical research and development on a higher-level autonomy perception system. The perception module interfaced with and controlled various sensors, including scanning LiDARs, cameras, and inertial sensors. To perform object detection, we created our own dataset and developed a pipeline to transform the dataset into annotations compliant with Pascal VOC. These annotations were then used to train an efficient object detection SSD Mobilenet model for live detection of traffic signs on Nvidia's Jetson Xavier NX. We also implemented blob and color detection using OpenCV to identify the state of stoplights. The research paper was accepted and presented at the ACM MobiArch 2022 conference.

Tags: Python, PyTorch, OpenCV, Deep Learning

Instance Segmentation App

Supervisor: Prof. Mohit Gupta, Dept. of CS, UW-Madison | Jan - May 2021

We built an app that enabled users to perform instance segmentation tasks. Users could upload their own images and obtain annotated results. For each object detected in the image, the annotation included a bounding box, a class label, and a binary mask that segmented the object from the background. The Mask R-CNN model was implemented using the Detectron2 package from Facebook AI Research. We offered multiple modes to allow users to specify the types of objects they were interested in, achieved by using multiple models trained on different datasets such as COCO, LVIS, and Cityscapes. Furthermore, we provided interactive functionality powered by GrabCut, allowing users to iteratively refine the segmented results. This was implemented using the OpenCV package.

Tags: Python, Pytorch, OpenCV, Computer Vision

Business Success/Viability Forecast Analysis

Supervisor: Prof. Daniel Pimentel-Alarcon, Dept. of Biostatistics, UW-Madison | Sep - Dec 2020

The incidence of COVID-19 posed a significant challenge to businesses across various industries. Some businesses thrived while others closed. Thus, it became more important than ever for business owners, customers, investors, and others to know which businesses would survive. In this project, we constructed a comprehensive dataset, including both normal business and COVID-specific information, by utilizing the Yelp dataset in combination with other external sources. We then presented various classifiers that could be used to predict whether an American business would survive the pandemic, and analyzed their performance using a training and evaluation pipeline. SMOTE was integrated into the pipeline during training to address class imbalance in the dataset. Multiple score metrics were used to assess model performance over both classes (open and closed businesses). Our test set results showed that model performance varied depending on the metric. We also determined the features most influential in predicting whether a business would survive. The analysis of feature significance revealed that certain COVID-19 features in our dataset, such as Grubhub, COVID Banner, and Call_To_Action_enabled, played a vital role in determining if a business remained open.

Tags: Python, Scikit-Learn, Machine Learning
